# 15 August 2010 — the overflow incident, dated from the record

**20 September 2026.** The one failure the constitution's `failure cited` column names is block
74638: a transaction whose two outputs summed past a signed 64-bit accumulator and minted
184,467,440,737 BTC under the January rule, which checked each output for a negative value and
nothing else (`OBL-C-0003`, executed in `derivatives/overflow/`). This note dates the event from
three records that do not depend on each other: the commits, the author's posts of that night, and
the chain's own timestamps. Findings register: `OBL-F-0031`. It attributes no motive and corrects
one common word: nothing was rolled back. A corrected release was shipped, and its chain won.

Experimental laboratory research, in progress. Not money, not advice, no warranty (`RIGHTS.md`).

## What the three records agree on

- **The failure and the fix were the same day.** The bad block was mined in the 17:00 hour; the fix
  was posted as code by 21:00, committed as r132 that evening, and released as 0.3.10 before midnight.
- **The corrected chain's block 74638 is timestamped 23:53:59**, almost seven hours after 74637.
  That gap is the record of the fork: for those hours the height was held by a block the corrected
  rules reject.
- **Two rules entered around the incident, and one of them was the response.** `MoneyRange` and the
  output-sum check are the response (`d4c6b90ca`, message describes it). The checkpoint at 74000, the
  script caps and the disabled opcodes are in `4bd188c43`, committed at 21:05 the same evening under
  the message "misc changes"; the author's post 370 calls the checkpoint "the most recent security
  lockin". The record dates them to the same day; it does not say which was written first.
- **What "overtook" means.** The author's posts describe the mechanism twice: nodes on the corrected
  release reject the overflow block, build on 74637, and once their share of generation exceeds the
  rest, their chain becomes the longest and every node reorganises to it (posts 379, 381). No block
  was removed by decree; the corrected rules made the old chain lose. Transactions in the old chain
  were "reincorporated into the fixed chain"; generated coins from the old chain after 74638
  "disappear" (379).

## What this note does not claim

No claim about who mined the overflow block, or why; no claim that 74691 is the exact overtaking
height (that figure is in secondary accounts and is cited here only as a chain timestamp); no
position on the response. The word "rollback" is not used, because the record shows a competing
chain winning under new rules, which is a different thing.

**Corrections to this note are published, dated, and not made silently.**
